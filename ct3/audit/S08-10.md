## 3. Three ranked findings

### 1. `challenge` never re-evaluates staleness, and its trace says it did

`_claim` computes `stale` at validation time; `challenge` copies each existing evidence
row verbatim, so the 90-day test is never re-run against the challenge's own clock — yet
the trace it writes dates the decision at that clock.

```
v1@2025-06-01:    INSUFFICIENT 0.0  stale= [True, True, True, True, True, True, True]
v2@2026-10-25:    INSUFFICIENT 0.0  stale= [True, True, True, True, True, True, True]
fresh@2026-10-25: PARTIAL      0.25 stale= [False, False, False, False, False, True, False]
v2 basis:      ['ASSESSMENT_VALIDATION', ...]      # all 7 rows
v2 row evaluated_at: ['2025-06-01T00:00:00Z', ...] # all 7 rows
v2 decision_evaluated_at: 2026-10-25T00:00:00Z
```

Same corpus, same clock: `challenge` says INSUFFICIENT 0.0, `assess_hypothesis` says
PARTIAL 0.25, a 16-month gap between the row times and the decision instant sits inside
one trace, and every carried row is labelled `ASSESSMENT_VALIDATION` although nothing was
validated at that instant in this version. Because `main.py:153-163` persists the revised
claims as the current revision, the API serves the verdict the engine itself would
contradict on reassessment. Fix: recompute `stale` for carried rows against `now` (that is
not a rewrite of quote/stance/source), or introduce a basis value such as
`CARRIED_FROM_PRIOR_VERSION` and stop calling the decision instant the evaluation instant.

*Counter-observation*: 545-546 says a challenge must not rewrite existing rows, and
preserving `stale` satisfies that literally. The tension is real, which is why the finding
is “the contract is incoherent”, not “preserve is wrong”: the same comment does not say the
verdict may then disagree with a fresh assessment, and no test pins either reading.
*Discriminator, run*: carry-forward predicts v2's flags equal v1's for the 7 pre-existing
rows; recomputation predicts 6 fresh like `fresh@2026`. Carry-forward held.

### 2. Six refusal causes share `invalid_assessment`, and over HTTP the only reachable quote case is the one whose message is false

535/542/544/559 all report the class default. The route pre-empts two of them
(`main.py:143` 404 for unknown/restricted, `main.py:145` 422 for an absent quote), so a
real request can only reach 544 by sending a quote that **is an exact substring** and
shorter than 3 characters after strip — answered with “Challenge quote must be an exact
source substring”, which is not what went wrong — or 559 by naming a record this claim
never depended on, which needs a different correction. A client cannot separate “fix your
quote” from “fix your claim”, and neither can an operator reading logs.

*Second half*: splitting the codes in `engine.py` alone is invisible. `main.py:162-163`
returns `str(exc)` and never reads `.code`; only `evaluate.py` does. The fix is the engine
plus the route's mapping, and the price is that 422-for-everything becomes several status
codes — a client-visible change.

*Deliberate and correct*: 542's single message for unknown-vs-restricted is a non-disclosure
decision pinned by `test_challenge_refuses_a_restricted_record`. The finding is about the
code, and about 544, not about that conflation.

### 3. `malformed_output` at 445 reports our own validator crash as the provider's bad output

434, 437 and 445 share `malformed_output`, and 445 is the `except Exception` around
`validate_assessment` — it fires when the validator raises something that is not an
`AssessmentError`, i.e. a fault in our code. Between that code and
`main.py:28-30`'s constant 503, a bug of ours becomes “the provider is unavailable or
returned invalid output”: wrong owner, wrong retry action, no diagnostic. Exactly the shape
of the slice-1 fix where an internal accounting fault was reported as
`request_too_large`. Suggested split: `internal_validation_fault`, or let it surface as a
500 rather than blame the provider.

*Limitation*: 445 is **read-only** in this audit. I did not construct a validator fault that
reaches it, so this finding is `Read`, not `Observed`, and it ranks third for that reason.
